Chapter 1

The Geometry of Faces: Maximum Variance vs. Class Separation

In computational face recognition, representing high-dimensional pixel spaces ($92 \times 112 = 10,304$ features) directly causes severe computational bottlenecks and susceptibility to pixel-level noise. Linear subspace learning methods find a projection matrix $W \in \mathbb{R}^{D \times k}$ ($k \ll D$) that captures the underlying identity structure. However, their foundational optimization criteria differ fundamentally:

1. Principal Component Analysis — Maximize Total Sample Variance (Unsupervised Eigenfaces)
$$ \max_{W} \operatorname{Tr}\left( W^T S_T W \right), \quad \text{where } S_T = \sum_{i=1}^N (x_i - \mu)(x_i - \mu)^T $$
2. Truncated SVD — Optimal Rank-$k$ Frobenius Approximation (Eckart-Young-Mirsky Theorem)
$$ \min_{\hat{X}, \operatorname{rank}(\hat{X}) \le k} \| X - \hat{X} \|_F, \quad \text{where } X \approx U_k \Sigma_k V_k^T $$
3. Linear Discriminant Analysis — Maximize Between-to-Within Scatter (Supervised Fisherfaces)
$$ \max_{W} \frac{\det\left( W^T S_B W \right)}{\det\left( W^T S_W W \right)}, \quad \text{where } S_B = \sum_{c=1}^C N_c (\mu_c - \mu)(\mu_c - \mu)^T $$
💡 The Fundamental Biometric Insight

Total pixel variance does not equal identity discriminability. PCA eigenfaces capture directions of greatest contrast—which are overwhelmingly dominated by ambient lighting, cast shadows, and background gradients. In stark contrast, LDA uses ground-truth identity labels to mathematically compress intra-person variations (facial expression, smiling, eyewear) while stretching the distances between different individuals.

Why Supervised LDA Dominates Every Component Count: The red curve representing LDA sits strictly above PCA and SVD across the entire spectrum. Even under extreme low dimensions ($k=5$), LDA delivers 83.15% (+2.8% ahead of PCA). By $k=39$, LDA hits 95.20% ± 1.69%, beating both unsupervised models.

By discarding 10,265 dimensions of high-frequency noise and non-discriminative background pixels, LDA functions as an intelligent denoising filter while achieving a 264.2× compression ratio.

🔍 Observation: Why PCA and Truncated SVD Are the Same Everywhere

Across every dimension of our empirical investigation—1-NN identification accuracy (94.55% vs 94.60%), Linear SVM classification (exactly 95.80% for both), reconstruction MSE ($0.012803$ at $k=5$, $0.007421$ at $k=30$ for both), 2D subspace cluster topology, inference latency ($\sim 13\ \mu\text{s}$ per face), and template memory (156 bytes)—PCA and Truncated SVD perform virtually identically. This is governed by four fundamental linear algebraic and biometric principles:

Pillar 1: Algebraic Equivalence
$$ X_c^T X_c = V \Sigma^2 V^T = (N - 1) S $$
For mean-centered face matrix $X_c \in \mathbb{R}^{N \times D}$, its sample covariance matrix is $S = \frac{1}{N-1} X_c^T X_c$. SVD factorizes $X_c = U \Sigma V^T$. Expanding the covariance yields $S = \frac{1}{N-1} V \Sigma^T (U^T U) \Sigma V^T = V \left[\frac{\Sigma^2}{N-1}\right] V^T$. Because $V$ is orthonormal and $\frac{\Sigma^2}{N-1}$ is diagonal, this is the exact spectral decomposition of $S$. Thus, the right singular vectors $V$ of SVD are strictly identical to the eigenvectors of PCA covariance, and $\lambda_j = \frac{\sigma_j^2}{N-1}$. On centered data, PCA is literally SVD.
Pillar 2: DC Shift Invariance in 1-NN
$$ (z_a - z_b)_1 \approx (x_a - x_b)^T v_1 \approx 0 $$
Standard Truncated SVD operates on uncentered data $X = X_c + \mathbf{1} \mu^T$. Because pixel values are positive, the grand mean face $\mu$ has massive magnitude (carrying energy $\sigma_1 \approx 660.06$). SVD's 1st singular vector $v_1$ aligns with this DC illumination centroid. Vectors $v_2$ through $v_k$ span the exact same variance subspace as PCA's $v_1$ through $v_{k-1}$. In 1-NN classification using Euclidean distance $\|z_a - z_b\|_2$, the constant projection onto the mean face subtracts out in vector differences. Pairwise distances remain isometric, finding the exact same nearest neighbors everywhere.
Pillar 3: Eckart-Young-Mirsky Theorem
$$ \min_{\hat{X}, \operatorname{rank}(\hat{X}) \le k} \| X - \hat{X} \|_F $$
The Eckart-Young-Mirsky theorem proves that Truncated SVD provides the globally optimal rank-$k$ matrix approximation minimizing Frobenius error $\|X - \hat{X}\|_F$. PCA solves the identical problem for deviations around the empirical mean face. Because facial images reside on an intrinsic low-dimensional manifold governed by orthogonal geometric variations, both algorithms project onto identical principal hyperplanes, yielding identical test reconstruction MSE ($0.012803$ at $k=5$; $0.007421$ at $k=30$).
Pillar 4: Snapshot Duality & Cost
$$ \mathcal{O}(N^2 D + N^3) \implies \sim 13\ \mu\text{s Latency} $$
Both algorithms utilize the Sirovich & Kirby (1987) snapshot method: instead of constructing the intractable $10,304 \times 10,304$ covariance matrix, both diagonalize the $200 \times 200$ Gram matrix $K = X X^T$. At inference time, both project a face image via a single dense matrix-vector product $x^T W_k$ ($10,304 \times k$ FLOPs), resulting in identical $12.7\text{--}13.2\ \mu\text{s}$ projection latencies and identical 156-byte storage footprints per face template.
Summary Insight: PCA and Truncated SVD are two mathematical viewpoints of the same linear subspace. Whether formulated as covariance variance maximization (Hotelling 1933 / Turk & Pentland 1991) or matrix low-rank factorization (Beltrami 1873 / Eckart & Young 1936), they extract identical geometric bases. Consequently, they yield equivalent recognition accuracy, reconstruction fidelity, and computational efficiency across every benchmark.

Face Reconstruction: Image Synthesis vs. Biometric Discrimination

While LDA dominates classification, can it reconstruct the original face image? No. Subspace methods have completely divergent mathematical objectives:

Figure 3: Visual reconstruction of held-out test faces (Subject 1 and Subject 10) comparing PCA and SVD across k = 5, 10, 20, 30.
Figure 4: Reconstruction Mean Squared Error (MSE) and Relative Frobenius Error (%) on held-out test faces as a function of k.
⚠️ The Reconstruction vs. Discrimination Trade-Off

PCA and SVD are mathematically guaranteed by the Eckart-Young-Mirsky Theorem to produce the optimal low-rank matrix approximation that minimizes Frobenius pixel error. As shown above, reconstruction MSE drops from 0.0128 (k=5) to 0.0070 (k=39), with fine features (eyes, nose, eyeglasses) resolving faithfully. LDA, however, projects along axes that explicitly collapse intra-class variance. Because intra-class variance is necessary to render individual expressions and textures, LDA cannot faithfully reconstruct images without a hybrid PCA basis.

Chapter 5

Cumulative Explained Variance: Truncated SVD vs. PCA Comparison

How rapidly do linear subspaces capture the physical energy of face images, and how does uncentered Truncated SVD compare against mean-centered PCA across varying component counts?

Figure 5: Upper: Cumulative explained variance ratio (%) for PCA (blue) and Truncated SVD (green dashed) with 90% (k=75) and 95% (k=114/115) energy thresholds and LDA capacity mark (k=39). Lower: Absolute variance advantage Δ(k) = PCA − SVD.

Why PCA Leads at Low Components ($\Delta = +1.73\%$ at $k=5$): In standard uncentered Truncated SVD, the very first singular vector ($\sigma_1 \approx 660.06$) aligns directly with the grand mean face vector, absorbing the global DC illumination offset. Consequently, only singular vectors 2 through 5 capture genuine cross-sample variations, yielding 46.64% cumulative variance at $k=5$. In contrast, PCA explicitly subtracts the empirical sample mean $\mu$ first, allowing its leading 5 eigenvectors to focus purely on centered variance, reaching 48.36% (+1.73% advantage). As $k$ increases, this initial offset dilutes rapidly: by $k=20$, the difference drops to 0.34%, and by $k=39$, PCA (81.58%) and SVD (81.39%) differ by less than 0.19%.

Exact Energy Retention Benchmarks: Both methods cross key biometric information thresholds at nearly identical component counts:

  • 90% Energy Threshold: PCA reaches 90.14% at $k = 75$; Truncated SVD reaches 90.03% at $k = 75$.
  • 95% Energy Threshold: PCA reaches 95.03% at $k = 114$; Truncated SVD reaches 95.05% at $k = 115$.
  • 100% Full Subspace Energy: PCA reaches 100.00% at its rank ceiling of $k = 199$; SVD reaches 100.00% at its uncentered rank ceiling of $k = 200$.

The Biometric Variance Paradox: At $k=39$, PCA and SVD retain ~81.5% of total dataset variance, while LDA is mathematically capped at $k=39$. Yet LDA at $k=39$ achieves 95.20% identification accuracy, outperforming PCA at $k=150$ (which retains >97.8% variance but reaches only 94.65% accuracy). This conclusively proves that retained variance measures signal reconstruction energy—not identity class separability.

Total Number of Possible Components: Mathematical Rank Derivation

How many non-zero components can PCA, Truncated SVD, and LDA physically generate on the ORL Face Database? The cards below detail the exact linear algebraic limits:

PCA (Eigenfaces)
199
$$k_{\max}^{\text{PCA}} = \min(N_{\text{train}} - 1, D) = 200 - 1 = \mathbf{199}$$
Centering Constraint: Training data consists of $N = 200$ face images. Subtracting the sample mean vector imposes the linear constraint $\sum_{i=1}^N (x_i - \mu) = 0$, consuming exactly 1 degree of freedom. Thus, the sample covariance matrix has rank at most 199. All eigenvalues beyond $k = 199$ are identically zero ($\lambda_k = 0$).

Full Dataset Limit ($400$ images): $\min(400 - 1, 10,304) = \mathbf{399}\text{ components}$.
Truncated SVD
200
$$k_{\max}^{\text{SVD}} = \min(N_{\text{train}}, D) = \min(200, 10,304) = \mathbf{200}$$
Uncentered Full Rank: Standard Truncated SVD factorizes the raw uncentered data matrix $X$ without mean subtraction. Because no centering constraint is applied, the matrix maintains its full algebraic rank of 200.

The first singular value ($\sigma_1 = 660.06$) absorbs the global DC illumination offset ($\approx \sqrt{N}\|\mu\|$), leaving $199$ singular vectors for facial variation.

Full Dataset Limit ($400$ images): $\min(400, 10,304) = \mathbf{400}\text{ components}$.
LDA (Fisherfaces)
39
$$k_{\max}^{\text{LDA}} = \min(C - 1, D) = 40 - 1 = \mathbf{39}$$
Between-Class Scatter Bound: For $C = 40$ subject identities, the between-class scatter matrix $S_B = \sum_{c=1}^C N_c (\mu_c - \mu)(\mu_c - \mu)^T$ is constructed from the $40$ class centroid vectors that sum to zero relative to the global mean. Hence, $\operatorname{rank}(S_B) \le C - 1 = \mathbf{39}$.

It is mathematically impossible to extract a 40th discriminant component regardless of sample size or pixel resolution.

Full Dataset Limit: Always $\mathbf{39}\text{ components}$ (strictly independent of $N$).
📌 Key Takeaway for Biometrics Engineers

While PCA and SVD can scale up to 199 and 200 components on the training set (capturing 100% of variance), LDA achieves its peak 95.20% accuracy at its theoretical maximum of only 39 components. This makes LDA not only superior in accuracy, but dramatically more compact, requiring only 156 bytes per face template compared to 800 bytes for full-rank PCA/SVD.
